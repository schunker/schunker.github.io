Physics at Newcastle

Our researchers and students investigate fundamental questions across physics, with particular strengths in astrophysics, solar and stellar physics, magnetism, space environments, computation and data-driven science.

Research is closely connected to teaching. Students can move from undergraduate projects into Honours and Higher Degree by Research, gaining experience with real scientific questions, modern datasets, numerical modelling and computational tools along the way.

Research

Understanding physical systems through complementary observations, models and simulations.

Solar Physics

The Sun is our nearest laboratory for understanding magnetic activity. Research includes the solar interior, magnetic-field generation, active regions, flux emergence, solar flares, coronal mass ejections and the solar atmosphere.

Stellar Physics

Stars provide laboratories for testing how rotation, magnetism, convection and evolution interact. We use observations and modelling to understand stars across different stages of their lives.

Seismology & Waves

Waves carry information from otherwise inaccessible regions of stars. Helioseismology and asteroseismology allow us to probe stellar interiors, rotation and structure using measurable oscillations.

Space Weather

Solar activity drives a changing space environment around Earth. We investigate how magnetic energy is stored, released and transported, and how solar eruptions propagate through interplanetary space.

Computational Physics

Numerical simulations provide a way to test physical ideas that cannot be isolated experimentally. We combine simulation, high-performance computing and observations to investigate complex physical systems.

Data & Machine Learning

Modern physics produces enormous datasets. Statistical inference, machine learning and data analysis help us identify patterns, quantify uncertainty and extract physical information from observations and simulations.
